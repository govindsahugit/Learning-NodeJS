## 🧾 1. Public SLA (most common for products)

👉 You simply **publish it on your website**

* No signing required
* Applies to all users automatically
* Usually part of **Terms of Service**

Example:
Companies like Amazon Web Services, Google Workspace, Microsoft, Slack, and Zoho do this.

📄 Real public SLA examples:

* AWS Compute SLA → [https://aws.amazon.com/compute/sla/](https://aws.amazon.com/compute/sla/)
* Google Workspace SLA → [https://workspace.google.com/terms/sla](https://workspace.google.com/terms/sla)
* Microsoft Online Services SLA → [https://www.microsoft.com/licensing/docs/view/Service-Level-Agreements-SLA-for-Online-Services](https://www.microsoft.com/licensing/docs/view/Service-Level-Agreements-SLA-for-Online-Services)
* Slack SLA → [https://slack.com/intl/en-in/terms-of-service/service-level-agreement](https://slack.com/intl/en-in/terms-of-service/service-level-agreement)
* Zoho Mail (uptime commitment) → [https://www.zoho.com/security-faq.html](https://www.zoho.com/security-faq.html)

📌 How it works:

* User signs up → agrees to terms
* SLA is included → legally binding

## 🤝 2. Contract-based SLA (B2B / enterprise)

👉 Here it’s a **formal agreement**

* Included in:

  * contracts
  * MSAs (Master Service Agreements)

* Signed:

  * digitally (most common today)
  * sometimes physically

📌 Used when:

* Big clients
* Custom pricing
* Higher guarantees (like 99.99% uptime)

---

## 🧑‍💻 3. Internal SLA (team-level, not public)

👉 No legal document

* Defined inside the company
* Used for:

  * engineering goals
  * alerting
  * reliability tracking

Example:

* “API latency must be < 200ms P95”

---

## ⚖️ What makes it legally valid?

For public SLAs:

* Acceptance of Terms of Service = agreement
* No separate signature needed

For contracts:

* Signed agreement = binding

---

## 🧠 Practical takeaway (for you)

If you’re building something like your platform:

* 🚀 Early stage → Internal SLA (for learning + alerts)
* 🌐 Public users → Publish SLA page
* 🏢 Enterprise clients → Signed contract SLA

---

## ⚡ One-line clarity

> 📜 **SLA can be a webpage, a contract, or an internal rule — depending on how serious and formal your system needs to be.**
